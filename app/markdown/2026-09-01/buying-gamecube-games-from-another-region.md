# Buying GameCube games from other regions

This blog post was inspired by discovering that [Pokémon Box: Ruby & Sapphire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pok%C3%A9mon_Ruby_and_Sapphire#Pok%C3%A9mon_Box:_Ruby_and_Sapphire),
a legendarily rare and expensive GameCube game in the United States ([>$2,500 USD](https://www.pricecharting.com/game/gamecube/pokemon-box)),
could be purchased [complete-in-box (CIB) in Japan for $50 USD](https://www.pricecharting.com/game/jp-gamecube/pokemon-box).
That's a savings of $2450, or ~98% of the US price.
This is a somewhat extreme example: in the US market
Pokémon Box was only sold online
and at a single store: the Pokémon Center in New York City.

This big price difference between regions got me thinking:
what other games are cheaper in other regions and by how much?

Even more excitingly, [there is a mod patch for Pokémon Box](https://projectpokemon.org/home/files/file/4950-pokemon-box-ruby-sapphire-universal-version/) enabling the
Japanese version to communicate and trade Pokémon between
[US, JP, and PAL Game Boy Advance games](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Software_region). This means if you wanted
to use Pokémon Box functionality you could buy a Japanese copy for $50,
patch the software, and use the software as if it were from any region.

This fact carries beyond GameCube games, as you'd imagine.
Legend of Zelda: Minish Cap is [$90 for a loose US copy](https://www.pricecharting.com/game/gameboy-advance/zelda-minish-cap)
and [only $40 for a Japanese copy](https://www.pricecharting.com/game/jp-gameboy-advance/zelda-minish-cap).
Surprisingly it's not always the case that the US release is
cheaper than the Japanese release. For example, the [Sonic Gems Collection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sonic_Gems_Collection)
for GameCube costs [$55 CIB for a US release](https://www.pricecharting.com/game/gamecube/sonic-gems-collection)
compared to [$180 CIB for JP](https://www.pricecharting.com/game/jp-gamecube/sonic-gems-collection).

I suspect this is because the soundtrack for Sonic CD is different for
each region, JP Sonic Gems Collection carrying the far-superior
and sought after JP Sonic CD soundtrack. The US Sonic CD soundtrack
was included in the US and PAL Sonic Gems Collection releases.
The Japanese Sonic CD soundtrack was [released for the first time
outside Japan in Sonic Origins](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/?id=2825322428).

## Why shouldn't you buy other regions' games?

The primary reason is if you don't speak the language. Many PAL
GameCube titles include English as one of the many supported languages,
but this can't be said for NTSC-J. Most Japanese titles are exclusively
in Japanese, and I don't speak the language. For some games this is fine,
not knowing Japanese won't hamper games that aren't text-heavy, but
RPGs or story-based adventures will impact the fun-factor.
There are some modifications you can apply to translate the Japanese
text back into English.

Many consoles are region-locked, and thus require
either purchasing a matching region console, modifying a console to support all regions,
or dumping ROMs and using emulators (usually with a dedicated device or console modification).
So you can't use any region's software without at least some extra work
or hardware. The Nintendo GameCube has three main regions: NTSC-J (Japan), NTSC-M (Americas),
and PAL (and maybe NTSC-K (Korea)? I cannot tell if this is actually its own region).

I've gone the way of modifying my GameCube hardware with a solderless
mod-chip called the [FlippyDrive](https://docs.flippydrive.com/), but other [open source options
for a similar device are now available](https://github.com/makeo/PicoLoader).
This modification allows running any regions games on any region hardware.

If you are using unmodified hardware you may run into issues with
frame rate if you're mixing software and hardware from an NTSC region (US, JP, KR)
and the PAL region. I am not super knowledgeable in this area,
look around for solutions to this issue before buying.
